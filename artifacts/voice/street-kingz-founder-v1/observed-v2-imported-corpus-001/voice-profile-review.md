# Street Kingz Founder Voice Profile

Status: **Awaiting human review**  
Profile: `street-kingz-founder-v1`

## What your voice sounds like

Direct, conversational and practical. You normally talk to the customer as “you”, explain what something means in use, and are comfortable giving a clear opinion. The humour is blunt and self-aware, but the useful point usually stays easy to find.

## Strong patterns

- Address the customer directly and frame advice around what they need to do or decide. (25 sources)
- Use ordinary contractions and a mix of explanatory sentences with short emphatic follow-ups. (27 sources)
- Use first person naturally for a personal view, admission or recommendation rather than maintaining detached editorial distance. (26 sources)
- Explain technical or practical points through what the customer does next, using concrete sequences rather than abstract definitions. (22 sources)
- Translate a specification into its practical consequence in plain language. (19 sources)
- Humour is blunt, self-aware and conversational, often used to puncture overcomplication; use sparingly in polished articles. (20 sources)

## Moderate patterns

- Move openly from useful explanation to a direct product invitation; long-form copy should soften repeated hard CTAs without hiding ownership. (16 sources)
- Questions are used to introduce a problem, anticipate an objection or challenge an overcomplicated view. (15 sources)
- Qualify advice with candid uncertainty or a plain admission rather than formal evidentiary language. (5 sources)

## Spoken habits we should clean up in articles

- filler
- repetition
- abandoned sentences
- TikTok hook repetition
- routine swearing
- repeated get-yours CTAs
- video-dependent references

## Written tendencies

The existing written evidence is limited and deliberately instructional. It supports conditional recommendations, clear I/we ownership rules, a strict fact boundary and a less aggressive commercial tone than the videos. It does not yet establish a broad natural long-form rhythm.

## Words and phrasing that feel natural

- “Just saves a lot of time.” — practical payoff in plain words
- “But it did the job.” — candid, uncomplicated qualification
- “Done. Simple. Easy.” — short emphasis after a demonstration

These are examples of behaviour, not catchphrases to repeat. “Anywho”, “get yours” and strong language are common in the videos but should not become mandatory article mannerisms.

## Language that does not sound like you

- Detached phrases such as “the product details support that fit assessment”.
- Legalistic uncertainty such as “there is no reliable basis here”.
- Abstract SEO-summary language that restates criteria instead of helping someone decide.
- Corporate third-person references to Street Kingz when “I”, “we” or “our” would be honest and natural.

## How Street Kingz articles should read

A cleaner, considered version of the same communication: address the reader directly, explain the practical consequence, give a view, name the trade-off and move on. Keep natural contractions and occasional short emphasis. Remove transcript loops, filler, repeated hooks and repeated selling.

## First-person guidance

Use “I” for judgement or a transparent opinion and “we/our” for the business or its products. Do not force first person into neutral explanations. A voice pattern never authorises a personal experience claim.

## Commercial ownership guidance

Be open that Street Kingz sells the product. Explain where it fits after helping the reader understand the decision. Avoid pretending independence, universal-best claims and repeated hard CTAs.

## Humour guidance

Dry, blunt or self-aware humour can work when it makes the point easier or more human. Reduce routine swearing and do not manufacture jokes, anecdotes or founder experiences.

## Technical explanation guidance

Translate specifications into what changes for the customer. A definition without a practical payoff is not enough. Avoid talking down to the reader or turning a simple job into detailing theatre.

## Confidence and limitations

The spoken evidence is strong enough to establish recurring communication patterns: 24 transcripts and 4,205 words. Written adaptation remains less certain because the retained written corpus is only 409 words and is instruction-heavy. The transcripts were not checked against audio, and the profile still requires human approval.
