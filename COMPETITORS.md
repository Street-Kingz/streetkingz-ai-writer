1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the competitive environment around the Product.

It exists to prevent:

building capabilities that already exist cheaply or freely;

mistaking a crowded category for whitespace;

assuming an idea is differentiated without evidence;

copying paid competitors feature-for-feature;

overlooking adjacent products because they use different terminology;

making claims the Product has not earned;

repeating the earlier failure to identify materially relevant competitors such as Koala before architectural decisions were made.

This is a living evidence register, not a marketing document.

It should be used to answer:

What can an ecommerce owner already do for free?

Which free alternatives must the Product beat on usefulness?

Which paid products already provide capabilities within our scope?

Which adjacent products could move into our position quickly?

What does a competent human ecommerce SEO specialist already do?

What should we build, buy, integrate, benchmark or deliberately avoid?

What claims are supported by evidence?

What changes in the market would require a product or roadmap review?

COMPETITORS.md does not set the roadmap. It constrains and informs it.

This document distinguishes:

Verified capability: clearly documented in an official product page, help centre, app store or plugin listing.

Vendor claim: performance, usage or outcome claimed by the vendor but not independently verified during this research.

Independent evidence: credible third-party review, marketplace review or public dataset.

Unverified: insufficient evidence to rely upon.

features inside a broader platform rather than standalone products.

The correct response is a monitoring process, not a false claim of exhaustiveness.

4. Threat and Maturity Definitions

4.1 Threat level

Critical

Directly overlaps the Product's core customer journey, has meaningful execution capability, has platform/data advantage, or could make the Product irrelevant for a major segment.

High

Substantial overlap with either free intelligence or paid autonomous execution; should materially influence benchmarking and roadmap decisions.

Medium

Competes with one important layer, but does not currently own the full journey.

Watch

Emerging, poorly evidenced, narrowly adjacent or currently low-access, but strategically relevant.

Low

Useful context but not likely to displace the Product's core proposition.

4.2 Maturity

Established

Meaningful market presence, customer base, reviews, product history or proven platform distribution.

Scaling

Commercial product with substantial capabilities and evidence of active growth, but less entrenched than established incumbents.

Emerging

New or lightly validated product, often with few independent reviews.

Concept / unverified

Promising claims with inadequate public evidence of product quality, customer adoption or reliability.

5. Executive Verdict

5.1 The market is not empty

Nearly every individual capability in the Product vision already exists somewhere:

Search Console analysis;

business profiling;

site crawling;

keyword and SERP research;

content gaps;

prioritised action feeds;

AI content generation;

product/category optimisation;

internal linking;

technical fixes;

approval workflows;

CMS publishing;

rollback;

rank and AI-visibility monitoring;

ecommerce analytics;

product profitability;

stock and margin intelligence;

AI operators acting on connected business data.

We are not inventing commercially informed SEO, AI SEO execution or ecommerce decision intelligence.

5.2 “AI finds work and edits the website” is already commodity-bound

AYSA, Search Atlas/OTTO, SEOmatic, Inxy, Essiow, Frase and several newer WordPress/MCP products already cover significant parts of:

analyse → recommend → prepare → approve → deploy → monitor.

The Product cannot rely on autonomous editing as its conceptual differentiator.

5.3 “Free” is not unique

The market already includes meaningful free products:

Google Search Console and Insights;

Bing Webmaster Tools and Copilot;

Ahrefs Webmaster Tools;

Screaming Frog's 500-URL crawler;

Yoast, Rank Math, AIOSEO and SEOPress;

ThinkRank with BYOK and MCP;

Semantic.io's free site/GSC audit;

Essiow's free audits and internal-link/indexation utilities;

various free audit, llms.txt, schema and AI visibility tools;

general-purpose AI used with exports.

The free benchmark is not one weak competitor. It is a stack.

5.4 Complete free intelligence plus complete DIY guidance remains the clearest surviving position

Across the products researched, many offer:

a free trial;

a free audit;

limited credits;

a free plugin;

basic recommendations;

isolated utilities.

No researched product clearly makes the following its durable central promise:

Analyse the ecommerce business and organic landscape, prioritise what matters, show the complete evidence and reasoning, and give the merchant everything required to implement the recommendation properly themselves for free—then charge only when the merchant asks the software to perform the labour.

This is not proof that no such product exists.

It is the best-supported competitive opening found in this sweep.

5.5 Paid execution is not novel, but the monetisation boundary may be

Approval-first execution, one-click publishing and rollback already exist.

The intended distinction is not the existence of a button. It is:

advice-complete free intelligence;

no artificial withholding;

ecommerce-aware prioritisation;

plain-English education;

complete DIY route;

paid autonomous labour only;

structural separation between recommendation quality and execution revenue.

5.6 Commercial context is a quality requirement, not a moat by itself

Human ecommerce SEO specialists already connect search demand with:

products;

categories;

revenue;

conversion;

business goals;

catalogue structure;

stock or merchandising constraints where relevant.

Ecommerce analytics platforms already understand:

profit;

COGS;

inventory;

customers;

attribution;

product performance.

The Product must prove that combining these signals produces materially better decisions. It cannot claim the method is new.

5.7 The largest platform threat is native commerce AI

Shopify Sidekick Pulse already:

proactively analyses store data;

surfaces up to five suggested actions;

builds a task list;

learns from dismissals;

applies changes only after approval.

It is currently early access, but it demonstrates that ecommerce platforms can absorb recommendation and execution workflows into the platform itself.

WooCommerce provides a better initial wedge because no single native Woo product currently owns the same broad intelligence layer. That advantage may not last.

5.8 The largest architectural threat is MCP/BYOK commoditisation

ThinkRank and similar tools show that a free WordPress plugin can expose dozens of SEO actions to Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor, while the user funds the model directly.

This means the Product's value cannot merely be:

“AI can access and edit your WordPress SEO.”

The defensible work must be in:

evidence gathering;

business understanding;

opportunity selection;

prioritisation;

intervention choice;

safety;

reproducibility;

complete DIY teaching;

structured executors;

monitoring and learning.

6. The Competitive Position We Are Building Toward

6.1 The Product does not need to beat every paid platform

The project is being built by one founder in evenings and spare time.

Attempting feature parity with:

Ahrefs;

Semrush;

Search Atlas;

Polar;

Triple Whale;

Shopify;

mature agencies;

would be strategically stupid.

Paid competitors are:

quality benchmarks;

sources of product learning;

potential data/infrastructure providers;

warnings about commodity features.

They are not the full feature-completeness target.

6.2 The Free Product target

The Product should aim to be:

The most useful free way for an ecommerce business to understand what organic-growth work deserves attention, why, and how to implement it properly.

“Most useful” does not mean:

most keywords;

biggest database;

most dashboards;

most AI agents;

most reports;

most features.

It means the merchant leaves with a better decision and a complete route to action.

6.3 The Paid Product target

Paid execution should answer:

Is pressing this button better value than spending hours implementing the free recommendation myself?

It does not initially need to outperform every enterprise automation platform.

It must:

remove real labour;

preserve customer control;

prepare high-quality work;

make review easy;

deploy safely;

provide verification and rollback;

be more convenient than DIY or copying the recommendation between tools.

PART I — FREE ALTERNATIVES WE MUST BEAT

8. Google Search Console and Search Console Insights

Category: Free foundational competitor
Threat: Critical baseline
Maturity: Established
Price: Free

Verified capabilities

Google Search Console provides first-party performance data including:

queries;

pages;

impressions;

clicks;

CTR;

average position;

indexation and URL inspection;

site health and search diagnostics.

The newer Insights report provides a simplified view designed for users who are not data experts, including top and trending content and search changes.

Google has also introduced AI-assisted report configuration in Search Console, increasing the likelihood that natural-language interpretation becomes native.

Strengths

Authoritative first-party Google data.

Free.

Trusted and widely adopted.

No third-party approximation for actual Google impressions/clicks.

Essential for current visibility and change detection.

Increasingly accessible to non-experts.

Gaps relative to the Product vision

Does not understand product economics.

Does not independently research wider external demand in the same way as a full SEO data provider.

Does not create a commercially prioritised multi-source opportunity queue.

Does not provide a complete ecommerce-specific implementation plan.

Does not currently own autonomous WooCommerce execution.

Competitive implication

Any recommendation based on GSC must add value beyond restating:

high impressions;

low CTR;

position 8–20;

declining clicks;

trending query.

A user should not be able to reproduce the Product's main recommendation simply by opening Search Console Insights.

PART IV — HUMAN SPECIALIST BENCHMARK

36. What a Good Ecommerce SEO Specialist Already Does

Commercially informed SEO is not novel.

A competent ecommerce SEO specialist can already:

understand business goals;

identify priority products/categories;

map search demand to catalogue structure;

analyse GSC, analytics and conversion;

research SERPs and competitors;

distinguish product, category, comparison and informational intent;

plan internal linking;

decide whether to create, improve, consolidate or avoid content;

measure revenue and organic outcomes;

sequence work over months.

Some agencies explicitly describe revenue-first/product-first strategy rather than volume-first SEO.

Competitive consequence

The quality benchmark is not:

Can the Product produce plausible SEO text?

It is:

Would a strong ecommerce SEO specialist consider the recommendation sensible given the same evidence?

37. Human Pricing Benchmark

The exact price varies widely by:

geography;

competition;

site size;

content volume;

link acquisition;

technical complexity;

freelancer vs agency;

ecommerce specialism.

Current evidence

Ahrefs' current industry statistics report that:

78.2% of SEO professionals charge monthly retainers for some or all work;

$501–$1,000 is the most common monthly retainer band;

worldwide-market providers average materially more;

agencies and consultants generally charge more than freelancers.

Published UK examples found in this sweep include:

Whitehat SEO: from £1,967/month; SEO plus content from £2,967/month; audits £2,500–£7,500.

Straight Up Search ecommerce retainers: £1,395 and £1,950/month for published tiers.

Visionary Marketing Shopify SEO: £1,300–£2,500/month.

Other smaller/general UK offerings ranged from a few hundred pounds upward.

Conclusion

A quote around £2,000/month for serious managed SEO is entirely plausible.

The market gap is not that nobody provides the expertise.

It is that many early/growing ecommerce businesses cannot comfortably fund ongoing specialist strategy and execution.

The competitor research does not justify abandoning the Product vision.

Do not build toward

The Product must earn its right to exist by proving three things

Its free recommendation is more useful than credible free alternatives.
